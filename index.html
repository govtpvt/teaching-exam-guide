Government Teaching Jobs in India: Eligibility Tests and Recruitment

Becoming a government teacher in India is a two-step process that many applicants confuse: first you qualify an eligibility test, then you compete in a recruitment. Passing the first does not give you a job; it makes you eligible to apply. Teaching vacancies are listed with other notifications on the Govtpvt latest government jobs page.

School level

StepExamWhat it does
EligibilityCTET (central) or a state TETCertifies that you may be appointed to teach classes 1 to 5 (Paper I) or 6 to 8 (Paper II)
RecruitmentKVS, NVS, state education boards and selection commissionsSelects teachers against actual vacancies through a written exam and, for some posts, an interview or demonstration

The posts are usually named PRT (primary), TGT (trained graduate, classes 6 to 10) and PGT (postgraduate, classes 11 and 12). PGT posts need a postgraduate degree in the subject and generally do not require a TET.

College and university level

For assistant professor posts the eligibility tests are the UGC NET at the national level and a State Eligibility Test (SET or SLET) within a state. A SET is valid only in the state that conducts it. See the NET and SET page.

Professional qualification

A degree alone is not enough for school posts. Primary posts need a diploma in elementary education or an equivalent, and TGT and PGT posts need a B.Ed. The notification lists the exact combinations accepted; check it against your own degree and subjects. Details are on the TET explained page.

Admissions to teacher training

B.Ed and D.El.Ed seats are filled through state entrance tests and counselling. These appear under admission notices.

For any teaching exam, follow the admit card, answer key and result lists.
